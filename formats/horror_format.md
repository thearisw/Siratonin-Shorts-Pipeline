# Horror Format: rule-horror and misread monsters

> Theme file. Read **CLAUDE.md first** (general rules: §2 pacing, §3 ingredients, §4 writing style, §4b story logic, §15 titles), then this file. Where they differ, this file wins for this theme.

**Use for:** a world with one deadly rule, a monster, a copy, a curse. Something wants to hurt the narrator or the people they love.
**References (`references/horror/`):** Not Human (2 copies), Shadow Tag, The Lantern (6rants), Chosen Monster, Water Monster (ralph), Sleeping Is Deadly (revanrants). See `references/INDEX.md`.

## Quick numbers
- **Length 100–175 s**, narration **4.1–4.7 w/s** (house voice ~3.7–4 w/s is fine; don't go slower). ~400–650 words.
- **Pace stays fast to the very end.** No slowdown for the twist; it's broken up by counts, clock strikes, one-word lines.
- **Ending is bleak and quiet**, or a win with a chill. Never explain the mechanics after the twist (one line at most).
- §4b story logic is the hardest part of this theme: the twist must reinterpret the evidence, with ≥3 planted clues and 15–25 s of build-up. Fill in the §4b.14 self-check.
- Title shape: "In This World, [rule]!" (CLAUDE.md §15).

## Pick a template
- **A: Horror Rule + Twist.** The rule is real and the twist is that the bond was the trap, or the narrator is the monster.
- **D: Misread Monster.** The town believes the wrong thing about the rule. The ending shows the true mechanism.

### Template A — Horror Rule + Twist (Not Human, Shadow Tag)
| Beat | % of runtime | What happens | Example (Shadow Tag) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | 0–3% | The premise in one line | "In my town, everyone is born without a shadow." |
| **Rules of the world** | 3–18% | How the deadly rule works, its timing, and the penalty. Usually a time is involved | "Every night at sunset… whoever has it when the clock strikes twelve gets pulled into the floor. All that's left is their clothes." |
| **Who I am + the bond** | 18–25% | The narrator's trait, plus a **promise/pact** with a best friend or family member (this pays off at the twist) | "Twelve years, never tagged once." / "When we were six we made a pact: never ever tag each other." |
| **Escalation** | 25–60% | A chain of events with rising stakes and a **countdown** (population or clock times). Small dark-comedy beats | "Mrs Ruiz screamed, dropped her cupcakes, ran straight into the mailman…" "11:00… 11:10… 11:15, Maple Street. That's our street." |
| **Danger arrives** | 60–75% | It reaches the narrator: a chase, a near miss, or being pointed at | Chase over the roof, through the backyard and the creek; hide under the slide |
| **False relief** | 75–85% | It seems to be over. A warm moment with the friend | "Jonah threw his arms around me and laughed for the first time all night." |
| **Twist** | 85–97% | The bond was the trap, or the narrator is the monster. Show it slowly, with a count or step-by-step face change | Clock strikes 1…12 between lines; the hug was the tag |
| **Kicker / loop** | last 3% | A short, chilling quote or a callback to the hook line | "…whispering, 'You're it.'" / "In this town, one of us isn't human." |

Worked examples: `reference_scripts.md` (Not Human, Shadow Tag) and `reference_analysis.md` §19 (The Lantern).

### Template D — Misread Monster (Chosen Monster, Water Monster)
The hook states what the town *believes* about the rule. The ending shows the true mechanism, and every early fact is still true but means something new.
| Beat | % of runtime | What happens | Example (Chosen) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hook + rule as believed** | 0–8% | "In this world, ___." Plus the penalty, all by ~5 s. Include one "always" statement that will flip later | "Every family he knocks on disappears by morning." / "It always keeps its head above the water, watching everyone." |
| **How people cope** | 8–20% | The town's survival habit (which turns out to be the wrong one) | Lights off, pretend to sleep |
| **Rule break** | 20–27% | The monster does something it has never done, aimed at the narrator | It wakes in the daytime and points at Dantie |
| **Anomaly clues (≥3)** | 27–55% | Each is flagged with one odd sentence | "But he didn't hurt him." / "Desperate." / "He was crying." / "Strangely, it turned toward the rock." |
| **Escalation / real threat** | 55–72% | The danger gets bigger. An adult names a legend, or the stakes rise (the water is rising) | "Mr Abandoned… I thought it was just a story." |
| **Act on the clue** | 72–85% | The hero uses the anomaly before fully understanding it | Screams "Chosen!" to call him / uses the rock splashes to aim the monster |
| **Reveal** | 85–92% | Proof the belief was wrong | A hall full of the "disappeared", alive / the monster stranded on dry ground |
| **Understanding + kicker** | 92–100% | "That's when I finally understood." Then a 2–3 line re-read of the hook. It can end bleak (Chosen) or as a win (Water) | "Chosen was a warning. It meant he chose you. Run." |

Worked examples, beat by beat with times: `reference_analysis.md` §10 (Chosen Monster, Water Monster) and §19 (Sleeping Is Deadly).

## More devices from the newer horror reels (from reference_analysis.md §19–20)
### The Lantern and Sleeping Is Deadly
**The Lantern (6rants), Template A done like our Pineapple:**
- **A coming-of-age ritual as the rule:** "In my town, when you turn 13, you spend one night alone in the woods, and what comes back is never quite you." A rite of passage is a strong rule source because every kid in town eventually faces it.
- **A secret-word pact passes the test, then betrays.** The swapped friend knows "Thunderbird" because it "pulled it right out of Maya's head." This is the same device as our Pineapple ("You said it in your sleep"). It's proven, but we've used it now, so **don't reuse the secret-word twist in a future video**.
- **The trap is good advice from someone you trust:** "Blow it out. Hide in the dark." The grown-ups' rule was right all along.
- **The narrator stays alive inside the horror:** he's trapped in a tree, watching his copy get his welcome-back party, and speaks to the next victim: "Please Lily… don't let it go out." This ends as a warning to the viewer, not a quote.
- **The opening party comes back as the closing callback:** "Cake, balloons, welcome back" at 20 s and again at 145 s, now horrifying.
- **The tell is a small behaviour:** "They laugh a second too late." It's planted at 16 s, seen once in Maya at 57 s ("Only once."), and pays off at the twist.

**Sleeping Is Deadly (revanrants), misread monster plus underdog:**
- **The newcomer who doesn't know the rule:** "We moved here in the fall, and nobody told us the rule." The narrator breaks the rule from day one and survives, which is the mystery.
- **A lived-in rule montage:** six absurd details in 16 s ("The mailman delivers letters straight to people's pillow", "Grandmas in curlers snoring under the stars"). It's funny, and it sells the world fast.
- **A planted habit pays off:** "Goodnight walls, goodnight door" (24 s) becomes "Goodnight, house" at the climax (128 s). The weird harmless habit is the secret strength (the Template B plant, used inside a horror world).
- **Evidence planted before the reveal:** the blanket tucked in and the shoes lined up. "Then I understood" lists them back (§4b rule, done right).
- **A social cost escalates instead of a body count:** Ghost Boy, kids filming "Day three, Ghost Boy, still alive," neighbours dragging their beds away, then a town blockade.
- **The kicker is a joke at the antagonist's expense:** "Every house except the mayor's… He still sleeps on the lawn."
- **A themed call to action:** "Like and subscribe before your house hears how you talk to him." The CTA is part of the story, not generic.

### Hook patterns
1. **Cold open on the climax, then "You see, in this world…"** (Hadel, both reels). It's stronger than starting with the rule, because frame 1 shows the impossible thing happening to a family member.
2. **The rule plus a creepy result in one sentence:** "…and what comes back is never quite you." (Lantern)
3. **Absurd everyday image:** "In this town, everyone sleeps outside on their front lawn." (revanrants) It's a mystery hook without any danger stated yet.

## Visual and audio notes specific to horror
- Night sets, the blood-moon preset for danger, morning fog for the aftermath. Faces always lit even at night (CLAUDE.md §11).
- A twist face morph happens in steps: smile → wide grin → huge mouth → black void.
- Option from 6rants: 2–3 word phrase captions with only the stressed word in CAPS read calmer and creepier (`reference_analysis.md` §21).
- Light the scene from the story's own light source (lantern, streetlight) so a light going out changes the palette.
- Monsters never walk like people (CLAUDE.md §11).
