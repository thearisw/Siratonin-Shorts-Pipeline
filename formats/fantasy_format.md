# Fantasy Format: wholesome magic, mystery and rescue

> Theme file. Read **CLAUDE.md first** (general rules: §2 pacing, §3 ingredients, §4 writing style, §4b story logic, §15 titles), then this file. Where they differ, this file wins for this theme.

**Use for:** a magic rule with no real villain. The story is a mystery or a rescue, and the payoff is emotional: a reunion, a found family member, a pet who was there all along.
**References (`references/fantasy/`):** Walking House (Craftee), Pet Transform (ralph), 13th Stair and The Moon Returned Something (Hadel Films). See `references/INDEX.md`.

## Quick numbers
- **Length 100–155 s**, narration **4.1–4.6 w/s**. Hadel cuts very fast (0.77–0.83 s median shots).
- **Ending is warm**, with a callback kicker that gives a planted line a new meaning. A small creepy sting at the very end is allowed and sets up a Part 2 (13th Stair).
- **Plant the clue object on screen early** (the postcards at 28 s, before they matter at 71 s).
- Viewer-ahead mysteries are fine here: stack 4+ clues so the viewer feels clever, and end on a joke that uses one of them (§4b.12).
- **Never explain survival logistics.** One line at most (Moon's 12 s explanation is the weakest stretch in the set).
- Heartfelt endings slow to ~1.6 s shots in the last 20%.

## Pick a template
- **C: Wholesome Mystery.** A magic rule breaks once, and clues lead to a reunion or reveal.
- **E: Rescue + Sting.** A family member is taken into another world; cold open on the event, then "You see, in this world…".

### Template C — Wholesome Mystery (Walking House)
| Beat | % of runtime | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook + magic rule** | 0–13% | A strange rule that always resolves itself ("…it walks you back home. Every time.") |
| **Break the rule** | 13–15% | "For a hundred years, no house has ever ___. Until ours." |
| **Family loss + visual plant** | 15–22% | Someone is missing. Show the clue object early (the postcards on the fridge) |
| **Rule of three (places/events)** | 22–42% | "The next morning, ___." ×3, each a new striking location |
| **Clue clicks** | 42–60% | The narrator connects the plant to the events. "It wasn't lost. It was looking for him." |
| **Final clue + journey** | 60–80% | The last piece points to the destination |
| **Reunion / reveal** | 80–92% | Finding the missing person, a hug, everyone falling over |
| **Warm button + callback kicker** | 92–100% | A gentle magical gesture, then the planted line with a new meaning ("Wish you were here… but now, he is.") |

Pet Transform is a lighter variant of Template C. The clues are the pet's traits, repeated on a stranger (orange hair = orange fur, a white glove = a white paw),
plus a time pattern (she leaves at 3:00, the cat is on the porch at 3:05). The viewer guesses early on purpose, and the fun is watching the clues pile up.

Worked examples: `reference_analysis.md` §8 (Walking House, with times) and §10 (Pet Transform, with times).

### Template E — Rescue + Sting (Hadel: 13th Stair, Moon)
| Beat | Time | Example (13th Stair) |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold open on the climax** | 0–4 s | "I watched my little sister step on a stair that didn't exist, and the floor swallowed her whole!" The event happens first. |
| **"You see, in this world…"** | 4–13 s | The rule comes *second*, as an explanation. |
| **Old family wound** | 13–22 s | "Six years ago, my dad disappeared." Mom has a cover story ("Mom said he left us" / "died in a storm"). |
| **A clue only the hero gets** | 22–35 s | Lily's secret knock in the wall / a note in Dad's jacket: "Lighthouse. Low tide." |
| **Enter the other world** | 35–55 s | An endless staircase with doors carved with the names of the missing / a hidden door under the lighthouse with "DAD" scratched on it |
| **Reunion** | 55–70 s | "Older. Thinner. But I recognized him immediately." |
| **Clock escape** | 70–100 s | "When the clock strikes one, the staircase closes." / high tide. The last step vanishes and the hero grabs the parent's hand. |
| **Family whole** | 100–108 s | "For the first time in six years, our whole family was together." |
| **Sting** (13th Stair only) | last 8 s | "Eddie, Lily was never taught that knock." Three knocks under the stairs, then a fourth and a fifth. A happy ending with a chill that hints at a Part 2. |

- Hadel recycles this template: both reels have "Eddie", a dad missing for six years, a name scratched on a door, a clock or tide escape, and the "Older. Thinner. But I recognized him" reunion. **A reliable template can be reused with a new magic object.**
- **The sting is better than no sting.** Moon ends flat and spends 80–92 s on a dull explanation of how Dad survived ("maintenance station… supply tunnels… research shelter"). That is the weakest stretch in all four reels. **Never explain survival logistics. One line at most.**
