# Shoes — Template A: Horror Rule + Twist

**Title:** My Brother's Shoes Came Home Without Him 👟
**Theme:** horror (`formats/horror_format.md`), Template A. **Target:** ~110–120 s at ~4.2 w/s.
**Cover / shot 1:** a pair of empty sneakers walking up snowy porch steps, caption "shoes" on screen.

## Rules box
- **Trigger:** at sunset a pair of shoes walks up the street by itself and stops on its owner's porch.
- **Effect (as believed):** the owner vanishes at the next midnight. Bare footprints lead into the woods.
- **How people cope:** never touch the shoes, leave them on the porch, lock the door, pray. Chapel board counts the town each morning.
- **The truth (true from the start):** the shoes take whoever *touches* them (puts them on, carries them, hides them). The owner is usually the first to touch them, so the town never noticed.
- **Exception the twist needs:** none beyond the truth. Each victim's own shoes come home the next night (kicker).

## Script

**[HOOK]**
In my town, when someone is about to *disappear*, their *shoes* walk home first.

**[RULES]**
It always starts at *sunset*. A pair of shoes comes walking up the street. Nobody inside them. Step. Step. Step.
They climb onto a porch and stop. That's your house.
At *midnight*, the owner is gone. All that's left are bare *footprints* in the snow, walking into the woods.
So we have rules. Never *touch* the shoes. Leave them on the porch. Lock the door. Pray.
The chapel board counts us every morning. *140*.

**[WHO I AM + THE BOND]**
I'm Leo. My little brother *Max* is eight, and he wears my old sneakers. Two sizes too big.
When I gave them to him, I said, "What's mine is *yours*." He never stopped saying it back.
Grandma watched him lace them up and said, "Shoes don't care whose *feet* they're on." We laughed. We thought she meant hand-me-downs.

**[ESCALATION]**
The first night, Mrs Ruiz's *slippers* came home. She put them on to see if they still fit. At midnight, she was gone. Bare footprints, right into the trees.
*121*.
Then old Mr Hale's *boots* came home. He lived alone. He laughed, pulled them on and went out to shovel his porch. Midnight. Just a shovel, standing in the snow.
*97*.
Then baby Lily's tiny shoes came home. Her mom grabbed them and hid them in the closet, so Lily would never see them. At midnight, her mom was gone.
But *Lily* was still asleep in her crib.
Strangely, the footprints outside were *big*. Grown-up big.
The whole town said her mom went looking for her. Mr Ortiz sold hot cocoa to everyone watching the woods. Two dollars.
*80*.

**[DANGER ARRIVES]**
Then, at *9:00*, I heard it. Step. Step. Step.
Max's *sneakers* walked up our porch steps.
Max screamed. Mom grabbed him. I couldn't let him touch them.
So I picked them up myself, ran them out to the woods and threw them as hard as I could.
*10:00*. They were back on the porch.
*11:00*. Max was shaking under his blanket.
*11:30*. I shoved them inside my own boots, under my bed, and sat on top of it all.
That's when I noticed my *socks*. They were wet. Wet with snow I never walked in.

**[FALSE RELIEF]**
*11:58*. *11:59*.
*12:00*.
I ran to Max's room. He was still there. Snoring.
He was *safe*. I hugged him and laughed so hard I cried.

**[TWIST]**
Then my *feet* started walking.
I didn't tell them to. Down the stairs. Out the door. Barefoot in the *snow*.
Mrs Ruiz touched her slippers. Mr Hale touched his boots. Lily's mom touched Lily's shoes. Lily never did.
And neither did Max.
Shoes don't care whose *feet* they're on.
I grabbed the doorframe. My fingers slipped. One. By. One.
Max ran to the door. "Where are you going?"
"What's mine is *yours*," I whispered.

**[KICKER]**
The next night, a pair of *boots* walked up our porch. My boots.
And Max reached for them.

## §4b.14 self-check
**(a) Rules box:** above. The twist uses only "whoever touches"; never contradicted.
**(b) Planted clues (approx. times at 4.2 w/s):**
1. ~12 s rule "Never *touch* the shoes" (the town's own rule names the real trigger).
2. ~27 s Grandma: "Shoes don't care whose feet they're on." Repeated word for word at the twist (§4b.9 adult saying).
3. ~32–48 s every victim touches the shoes on screen (Ruiz puts them on, Hale pulls them on).
4. ~55 s anomaly: Lily still asleep in her crib (owner not taken).
5. ~58 s anomaly: the footprints were big. Grown-up big.
6. ~83 s anomaly: my socks are wet with snow I never walked in (right after I carried the shoes).
**(c) How the narrator learns:** observation (Lily in the crib, big footprints, wet socks) + hearing (Grandma's line). He only puts them together when his feet move (the proof), then lists the touches back.
**(d) Each example after the twist:** Ruiz: owner AND first toucher, fits both. Hale: same. Lily's mom: touched, taken; Lily: owner, didn't touch, stayed. Max: owner, never touched (Mom grabbed him), safe at 12:00. Leo: carried, threw, hid them: taken. Kicker: Leo's boots come home and Max, who always says "what's mine is yours", reaches.
**(e) Build-up before the twist:** 9:00 sneakers arrive (~65 s) → 10:00/11:00/11:30 → wet socks → 11:58/11:59/12:00 → relief → twist at ~95 s. ≈ 30 s of rising dread (≥15 s).
**Pact payoff (§4b.7):** "What's mine is yours": Leo shared Max's shoes, so he took Max's fate; the kicker turns the same line into the next trap.
**Fourth wall:** no viewer address, no questions to the audience.
